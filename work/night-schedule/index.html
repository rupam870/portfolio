Night Schedule, and the feature I cut

A four-part night routine that lives on my phone, and the story of cutting the best-looking feature in it.

RoleDesign and build, solo
ForMe, nightly
SurfaceMobile web, on the home screen
Built withOne HTML file, Cloudflare Pages

The problem

The plan only existed in my head.

Every night I wanted to do the same four things: practise communication and storytelling, read, meditate, journal. Ninety minutes, give or take. But the plan lived nowhere, so each evening opened with the same negotiation: which one, for how long, and am I actually doing this tonight? The negotiation usually won.

Habit apps were the wrong size for the problem. I didn't need help building a habit. I needed the four things already decided, and something to tell me when to stop.

Assumptions

Three guesses. One was right.

Right

The hard part is choosing, not doing.

Four things on one screen, each with a time already set. Nothing left to decide. That's the whole app, really.

Wrong

Seeing the timer will make me stop.

It doesn't. It just tells me the time is up. I still start storytelling and look up three hours later.

Wrong

A 21-day streak will keep me going.

It did the opposite. I had to build it to find that out.

Two screens, and that's the app

Pick a thing. Tap the dial. That's the entire interaction model; everything else is restraint.

The ring empties, it doesn't fill

You watch what's left, not what you've spent. Time remaining is what you actually care about at 11pm.

The dial is the button

Tap the circle to start, tap again to pause. One enormous target, nothing else to hit in a dark room.

The night runs until 4am

A session started at 12:30 counts for the evening before, because that's how nights work, whatever the date says.

The illustrations carry it

The same girl in all four, in watercolour, with slightly wobbly frames and a pencil permanently lost in her hair: talking to a room, reading with two cats, sitting with the incense going, writing the whole day down. She's the reason I open this instead of a stopwatch. The countdown label says FOCUS BABY, and that was never going to change.

What I cut

The prettiest feature was the one making things worse.

I built a 21-day tracker: a 7×3 grid under the cards, one cell per day, darker the more of that night you finished. Tonight got a black ring, and tapping any past day told you what you'd done. It was easily the nicest-looking thing in the app.

Why it went

It measured a completion I was never going to hit. I'd start the first session and still be at it three hours later, so most nights ended short of four. The grid stopped being a record and became a wall of near-misses: a calm app quietly turning into a report card.

The fix wasn't a redesign. The feature was answering a question I hadn't asked. It's gone, and the nightly ticks stayed. The data is still recorded in the background and backed up off the phone, so it's there if I ever want it. It just isn't on screen every night, making a case against me.

Where it stands

The negotiation is gone. Four decisions, made once, sitting on my home screen. That alone was worth the build, and it's the part I'd keep if I could keep only one.

The long session still runs long. A chime isn't a strong enough ending, and that's the next real problem: something harder to ignore than a sound, rather than a chart telling me afterwards that I failed.

And I open it because it's nice to look at. Two weeks in, that's not a soft feature. It's most of why the thing survived at all.

Every screen

One night, start to finish. There's nothing else in the app.
