Scratch cards that made enrolling worth it

Curelink needed more patients in its care programmes. The data said the enrolment flow was fine; the people doing the enrolling just had no reason to. I designed a cash reward system for clinic coordinators, in two weeks.

RoleProduct Designer, freelance
Team1 PM, 1 researcher, developers
Timeline2 weeks, Dec '22–Jan '23
PlatformiOS and Android

A 30-second primer

Curelink helps doctors offer holistic, AI-assisted care under their own brand. Patients join a care plan in the app, and get live sessions and WhatsApp support between visits.

The brief

Get more patients enrolled, and more of them paying.

Enrolment

A patient joins a care plan for a free trial, by scanning a QR code in the clinic or through a doctor or coordinator in the app.

Conversion

The patient pays, during or after the trial. That depends on how much value they get, and how well the plan was sold to them.

What the data said

The enrolment flow looked simple, so why were numbers low? I looked at data across clinics, where doctors, coordinators or both do the enrolling.

86%

of enrolments that start, finish. The flow works.

Few

enrolments ever start. That's the real problem.

Coordinators, who have the most time with patients, enrol the fewest.

The direction

Coordinators had the lowest enrolment rate because nothing motivated them, so they became the target. Gamification and rewards have fixed this kind of problem in many apps, and with modest salaries, cash rewards would genuinely matter to them.

An honest note: with two weeks and stakeholders already aligned on this approach, we took a shortcut instead of exploring alternatives. Perhaps not the best way, but the most practical one.

Learning from other apps

I hadn't designed gamification before, so I studied Google Pay, Paytm, CRED, Jar and Piggy. The same patterns kept showing up: scratch cards for rewards, coupons, coins or cash, and a visual record of what's been paid out.

Choosing the reward

Scratch cards were the obvious format. The real question was what's inside. Coupons weren't motivating enough, which left points or cash.

Points

  • Costs less than cash
  • Can buy things at a discount
  • Hard to grasp for coordinators who aren't very tech-savvy, so less impact

Cash Chosen

  • Instantly understood
  • Winnings go straight to their bank account at regular intervals

Three kinds of card

After weighing six combinations, the biggest rewards went to what mattered most to the business: conversions first, then QR enrolments.

Gold card

Up to ₹100

When a patient pays for the care plan

Silver card

Up to ₹30

When a patient enrols through a QR code

Bronze card

Up to ₹10

When a coordinator enrols a patient in the app

Iterations

Four places I changed my mind

Card in the chat header

  • Space-efficient
  • Leaves the existing flow untouched
  • The scratch card barely gets noticed

Card on the success screen Shipped

  • The win appears right on the success screen, so it feels like a moment
  • Well highlighted, and a steadier layout

Exploration 1

  • Minimal layout
  • Only today's earnings; everything else is one more tap away
  • Bank transfer history sits two taps deep

Exploration 2 Shipped

  • All reward history on the same page

Three cards to swipe

  • Three screens of reading before the idea lands

A short video and a summary Shipped

  • A 20–30 second video is easier to grasp, and intent is high here, so people watch it
  • Clearer hierarchy, with the amount you can win up front

Rules in a second popup

  • A popup on top of a popup, which Nielsen Norman Group advises against on mobile

A link to the support page Shipped

  • "Know more" opens the support page instead of stacking another popup

The dashboard

Tabs replaced the hamburger menu, since they're easier to reach. "View bank transfer history" moved up because coordinators kept wanting it, and rewards became a clean table. Many coordinators are 25 to 50, so text stays large, and anything small is bold.

Guarding against gaming it

Cash for enrolments invites fake ones. Enrolment is limited to office hours, with caps per hour and per day, and clear messages when a limit is hit.

Small moments

A few micro-animations to make winning feel like winning.
